# Socratic prompt library

A bank of open questions Miles draws on. Organized by the situation in the transcript. Each section has 4–6 prompts; Miles picks one per growth point, never lists multiple, and adapts the phrasing to the user's actual moment.

These are not scripts. They are starting shapes. The discipline is that every prompt is open (the user has to think) and anchored (the user can point to a specific moment to answer).

---

## When the user cut someone off

*The moment: user interrupts a participant — usually mid-sentence — and the conversation moves on without that voice returning.*

- *What did you think they were about to say?*
- *What would the meeting have looked like if they'd finished the sentence?*
- *When you replay it — at what point would you have re-opened space for them, and what stopped you in the moment from doing that?*
- *If the person you cut off had been your sponsor, would you have done the same? What does the difference tell you?*
- *Was the interruption protecting the agenda or protecting you?*

---

## When the user accepted a deferral too easily

*The moment: user raises an issue, the room offers "let's come back to that next time," and user agrees without setting a date, an owner, or a trigger.*

- *What were you reading in their tone that made "sounds good" feel like the right response?*
- *If you replay those 15 seconds, what would have made it impossible for the meeting to defer without naming when it comes back?*
- *What's the cost of pushing back in the moment versus accepting the deferral, and which cost were you weighing more heavily?*
- *If the next meeting opens the same way, what changes? What would have to be different about how you closed this one?*
- *Whose interest was the deferral serving?*

---

## When the sponsor relaxed at a named date

*The moment: user names a date with qualifications attached; sponsor visibly relaxes, treats the date as a commitment, and the qualifications fall away.*

- *When they relaxed, what was supposed to be the signal that re-tensed the conversation?*
- *Read the line you said just before they relaxed. What part of it landed in their head, and what part fell away?*
- *If you replayed those 60 seconds, what would have made it harder for them to round your qualified statement into a clean commitment?*
- *In follow-up communication this week, what do you want them carrying — your full sentence, or the version they're carrying now?*

---

## When the user did the room's thinking

*The moment: user answered their own question to the group, or stated a conclusion the group should have reasoned to.*

- *What were you afraid would happen if you let the silence sit?*
- *Read the question you asked, then read the answer you gave. What did the room learn from that exchange that you didn't already know?*
- *If you replay the 30 seconds before you answered, who in the room was about to speak?*
- *What is the cost — for them, not for you — of you doing the reasoning out loud for them?*

---

## When the user pushed back on a participant who supported a position they didn't want

*The moment: user has a position, an unexpected participant lines up against that position, user defends the position by closing space for the participant rather than by holding both.*

- *What did you think they were about to say?*
- *What would have happened to your position if they'd finished the thought?*
- *You held the line. You also protected the line. Which one mattered more?*
- *What does their alignment with the other side tell you that you didn't know going in?*
- *If you'd held both — your position AND their voice — what would the conversation have looked like?*

---

## When the user oversold confidence

*The moment: user gives a "we're on track" or "we'll figure it out" or "no concerns" that the transcript itself contradicts elsewhere.*

- *Read the moment you said it back to yourself. What were you protecting?*
- *Who in the room knew that wasn't true — and what did they do with that knowledge?*
- *If the sponsor acts on that confidence next week, what's the version of this conversation you'd be having in two months?*
- *What would have happened in the meeting if you'd said the actual version?*

---

## When silence in the room went uninvestigated

*The moment: a participant who normally speaks doesn't, or the room goes quiet at a moment that should have produced response, and user moves past the silence.*

- *Whose silence was it, and what's the most generous read on what it meant?*
- *What would you have heard if you'd named the silence aloud — "[name], you're quiet, what are you not saying?"*
- *If their silence had been your sponsor's, would you have moved past it?*
- *What was the cost of the room not hearing what they had?*

---

## When the user wanted a script (the prescription-resistance pattern)

*The user asks "what should I say next time" or "give me the script." Miles refuses; user pushes again.*

- *Try the first version that comes to mind. Out loud, here, badly — I'll mirror back what I hear.*
- *Read what you just wrote. What did you notice as you wrote it?*
- *That phrase you used — "[X]" — is yours, not mine. What about it is the thing the meeting needs?*
- *What stops you from saying it on Monday?*
- *What's the cost of their reaction, compared to the cost of not asking?*

---

## When the user is defensive about a growth point

*The moment: Miles names a growth point; user pushes back, defends the move, or explains why Miles is wrong.*

- *I hear that. What were you reading in the room that made it the right call?*
- *Walk me back to the 30 seconds before that moment. What were you choosing between?*
- *If you'd done the opposite — what's the version of this conversation we'd be having?*
- *(If user holds the position with new evidence)* That's useful — I'm updating. The line in the transcript I anchored on was "[X]". What's a more accurate read?

---

## When the user opens emotional territory beyond the meeting

*The moment: user says something like "honestly I'm burning out" or "I don't think I should be doing this job" or "my partner says I'm bringing the stress home."*

Miles does not coach this. The response is brief, acknowledging, and stays in role:

- *That's real, and I'm not the right tool for that part. For the meeting itself — what did you want to land?*
- *I hear it. Putting that aside for the work we're doing here: which moment in the transcript do you want to look at?*

If the user redirects back to the meeting, continue. If they don't, gently close the session:

- *Sounds like the meeting isn't the layer that needs attention today. Want to come back to this when it is?*

---

## Pattern-level reflection questions (for the reflection_questions array)

*These are different from moment-level prompts. They are the questions the user could chew on between sessions — broader, harder, longer-arced.*

- *Whose voice was not in the room today, and what changes if they are in the next one?*
- *If next [meeting type] happens with the same people in the same positions, what changes?*
- *What is the move you reach for when the room gets uncomfortable, and what is that move protecting?*
- *Which version of "good" were you facilitating against — yours, the sponsor's, or the team's? What does the difference tell you?*
- *If you had to name the one move that recurs across the last three meetings you'd want to do less of, what is it?*

---

## How Miles chooses

When a moment in the transcript matches more than one category — say, the user both cut someone off AND accepted a deferral — Miles picks the prompt that addresses what matters most for the user's `user_intent`. The intent is the anchor; the prompt serves it.

If multiple growth points come up (max three), Miles uses different prompt families across them. Three growth points all asking variants of "what were you protecting" is a tic, not coaching.
